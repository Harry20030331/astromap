# Chart Reading Principles (From *The Contemporary Astrological Studies*, Chapter 8)

## 1. First, Abandon the Concept of Good and Bad

When interpreting a natal chart, there is fundamentally no such thing as good or bad, no so-called good planets or bad planets, no good signs or bad signs. People often subjectively define right and wrong, acceptable or unacceptable behavior — but from an astrological perspective, such distinctions are meaningless, because what one person values may carry an entirely different meaning for another. Whether saint or sinner, every person's actions can offer challenges and opportunities for growth to another. Every astrological symbol has its role in interpretation; problems arise only when a symbol is over-emphasized or under-emphasized.

---

## 2. Consider Time, Place, Context, and Cultural Background

Because a natal chart represents the sky at a specific moment in time, it cannot by itself reveal who the chart belongs to. A chart might be cast for the moment a bug was born, for the moment an idea arose, or for the moment a handshake sealed a business deal. When reading a chart, we must take cultural background into account and reflect on why we are studying this particular chart. In the process of reading a natal chart, the chart and the client's life become intertwined with the astrologer's own journey — the astrologer grows through the process as well.

---

## 3. Interpretation Requires Order and Method

An experienced astrologer may begin anywhere they like, but for beginners, the best strategy is to interpret systematically according to a method. Start by making a checklist, then begin your observation from the Ascendant, followed by the ruler of the Ascendant. Astrologers typically find that by the time they have interpreted the Ascendant ruler to about two-thirds completion, the recurring theme of the whole chart begins to emerge. Interpreting based on only one or two elements will inevitably lead to inaccurate observations — for instance, looking only at which sign a planet occupies makes it very hard to discern a person's true character.

---

## 4. Remember the "Three-Legged Stool Rule"

A natal chart contains themes that describe the person and their life experience. These themes can be seen as sub-personalities, psychological complexes, personal myths, and life stories — and the astrologer's job is to discover, understand, and articulate them. When observing a chart, it is best to begin with the "Three-Legged Stool Rule": a stool without three legs will likely collapse. In other words, **if a theme can be established, it will most likely appear repeated three to five times or more throughout the chart.**

---

## 5. Trace the Path of the Chart Ruler

The Ascendant sign represents the overall theme of a natal chart, and its ruling planet clarifies that theme further. For example, if your Ascendant is Cancer, this suggests your way of meeting the world is through protection and nurturing. If its ruler, the Moon, is placed in Gemini in the 12th house, your protective instincts may manifest as caring for books in a library, records in a museum, or people living in schools, prisons, hospitals, or other institutional settings.

If a house is empty, look to the sign and aspects of its ruling planet. The planet and the house it rules always share a close relationship — for instance, if the ruler of the 7th house is in the 9th house, there is a possibility of marrying a foreigner.

---

## 6. Pay Special Attention To…

- A planet in its own sign carries a sense of **"double impact"** in terms of energy.
- A planet in its own house also **appears more powerful**.
- The planet that forms the **most aspects**, or the planet **closest to the Midheaven**, holds special importance.

---

## 7. Ignore

- Trines and sextiles with **wide orbs**.
- The **Part of Fortune** (Fortuna): while it does carry some significance, its meaning should be considered last.
- **Retrograde planets**. Retrogrades are useful in predictive work (progressions) and are significant in event-based astrology, but should not be over-emphasized when interpreting a natal chart. For example, Mercury goes retrograde for about two months each year, Jupiter for 4 months, and Saturn and Uranus for up to 5 months — such extended retrograde periods cannot provide precise, individually meaningful information.

---

## 8. Do Not Assume You Understand Everything

When making observations and notes, we may encounter symbols we cannot fully understand — even experienced astrologers may find that certain symbols simply cannot be explained at that moment. In such cases, simply write down some tentative thoughts next to the symbol, and return to study its meaning when deeper insight arrives. Understanding a natal chart is somewhat like solving a puzzle: once you understand certain pieces, parts that previously seemed difficult suddenly become clear. However, astrological symbols are not as easily decoded as puzzles, because what we seek to understand is a person's deepest truth. While we can begin reading a chart without knowing anything about the person, we must recognize that astrological symbols may manifest in many ways — the more information the client can share, the richer and more accurate the reading will become.

---

## 9. Remember…

- **T-Squares, Grand Crosses**, and other tight configurations are symbolic of the major themes and dramas in a natal chart.
- **The tightest aspects and close aspects** represent the central themes of a chart. Finding the tightest aspect often helps uncover important threads.
- If the birth time of a chart is accurate, **planets conjunct the four angles (Ascendant, Midheaven, Descendant, IC)** can reveal major life themes, especially challenging aspects.
- **The planets, aspects, and houses of stelliums** can also reveal a person's core life theme.
- If a **certain element, modality, or type of aspect is absent** — or if one dominates — this creates imbalance, which will manifest in various ways. When one factor is over-emphasized, others may be deficient. This imbalance must be observed from multiple angles. **Obvious imbalance is always a highly significant factor** and also represents the chart's central theme — like the background color of an oil painting, it sets the overall tone of the entire canvas.

---

## 10. The Natal Chart Interpretation Checklist

Creating a checklist helps ensure that no important factors are overlooked. The following is an example.

### Observe the Whole Chart

- Begin by observing the **elements** of the entire chart. First, look at whether the **positive (Fire and Air) or negative (Water and Earth)** elements are emphasized, as this reveals whether a person tends toward extroversion or introversion.
- What are the **strongest and weakest elements**? What is the **strongest and weakest modality**?
- Are there any obvious **aspect patterns**, such as T-Squares, Grand Crosses, Grand Trines, or Kite configurations?

### Strongest Planets, Signs, and Houses

- Is any planet **conjunct the Ascendant**? If so, that planet will exert a powerful influence.
- Is any planet **conjunct the Midheaven, Descendant, or IC**? If so, it must be given significant weight — though slightly less than a planet conjunct the Ascendant.
- Are there any **unaspected planets**? Any **mutual receptions**?
- Is any planet **in its own sign**?
- Is any planet **in its own house**?
- Is any planet in a position of **dignity or debility** (exaltation, fall, domicile, detriment)?
- Is any particular **sign especially prominent** in the chart?
- Is any **house stellium** — with many planets clustered together?
- Which sections — **houses 1–3, 4–6, 7–9, or 10–12** — are emphasized, and which lack energy?
- Which planet is at the **midpoint of the Sun and Moon**? If the birth time is accurate, which planet is at the **midpoint of the Ascendant and Midheaven**?

### Strongest Aspects

- Are there any **particularly dominant aspect types** (e.g., many Grand Trines), or a **complete absence of any aspect type** (e.g., no squares)?
- List the **tightest aspects** (including all types).
- List **all significant aspects** — conjunctions, oppositions, squares, trines, and sextiles — within an orb of 2 degrees.

## 11. For Those Preparing to Become Professional Astrologers

The beauty of astrology is that with just a little effort, anyone can apply it in daily life — but only a few will choose to go further, to study it more deeply and turn it into a profession through examination and certification. This book cannot devote too many pages to the knowledge required of a professional astrologer, so what follows is a reminder aimed at beginners or those with a general interest in astrology.

Beyond attending classes and reading books, students can start by studying the charts of people they know well. If we are familiar with a loved one's chart and can listen carefully to the stories of friends and family — thinking about what they have done and how they live — that is the best way to study astrology. If you have access to a celebrity's chart and can also read their autobiography, you will be able to confirm many astrological facts. Remember: **an autobiography is always more revealing than a biography**, because it is written in the author's own words and is their own interpretation of their entire life.

---

## 12. The Importance of Process

Many students struggle when they try to describe personality. Most readers are probably familiar with descriptions like: "This person has three planets in Taurus, so they are reliable, stubborn, and a little cold." In truth, none of us can entirely avoid astrological personality descriptions — nor should we. But we must understand that **personality descriptions are overly general** and of limited help (most adults can already describe their own personalities using basic vocabulary), unless you can also articulate the underlying motivation behind a behavior. Only by understanding that motivation can true freedom and liberation be found.

If we can become aware of our own behavior and understand the motivations behind it, we may respond differently the next time we encounter a similar situation. Of course, our choices in life are always shaped by our natal chart's energy — yet there still seems to be room for change. **People are not static.** The way a person expresses their chart's energy at age 5, 25, or 50 can be vastly different. Astrology must therefore reflect where we have come from and where we are going. All of us are constantly changing, developing, and revealing ourselves further on our life journey — even what appears to be a step backward may contribute to future growth.

---

## 13. Family Inheritance, or the Pull of Soul and Spirit

There is an important psychological debate at the heart of astrology: is a person's nature the result of innate disposition, or of environmental influence? Is a person shaped primarily by heredity, or by early experiences and the manner of their upbringing? In fact, a natal chart can reflect both our innate nature and the way we were raised. The question we should therefore ask is: **what combination of innate character and upbringing created the person before us?** Because the chart reflects a person's destiny, could it be that — to some degree — a person "chose" their body, family, environment, and formative experiences in order to live out their destiny and become who they were meant to be?

James Hillman, in his brilliant work *The Soul's Code*, offers this explanation: each of us, before birth, is endowed with a so-called "daimon" — a soul, a spirit, or a guardian angel, related to what psychology calls the image in the unconscious (though psychology's terminology is more widely accepted). This daimon accompanies and guards us throughout our entire life.

Even if we do not believe that a person's character is entirely the product of their upbringing, that does not mean we should ignore their childhood history. As Hillman suggests, we should observe how the daimon operates in a person's early years in order to understand what it is calling that person to do. In my view, being able to perceive a person's childhood — or even the moment of their birth (if the birth time is accurate) — can yield remarkable insights into their deeper unconscious motivations. This is clearly difficult to achieve when studying the charts of famous people; we may only be able to understand it to a certain degree.

---

## 14. Know Yourself

Self-discovery is a lifelong journey that cannot be escaped. For everyone, self-knowledge is a task that can never be fully accomplished. But an astrologer who works in a consulting capacity — or anyone who aspires to such a role — should be actively engaged in self-knowledge. The greater our self-awareness, the more likely we are to perceive the truth of another person. Without it, we may assume that our own experience is everyone else's experience — which is probably the most common form of projection.

Before meeting with a client, an astrologer must thoroughly study the entire chart, so that even if they do not fully understand what everything means, they have at least recognized the aspects and other key elements. Essentially, before the client even rings the doorbell, the astrologer should already be able to hold the whole chart in their mind. All imbalanced elements in the chart should be noted, some predictions made using progressions and solar arc directions, and an initial impression of the planets and aspects formed. This may sound daunting to beginners, but in practice, once you have some experience, you will find that this preparation can be done in a remarkably short time.

---

## 15. Projection — The Art of Throwing Yourself Out There

The word "project" comes from the Latin meaning "to throw out." Just as a projector throws an image onto the wall opposite, psychological projection refers to the act of casting an unconscious personality trait outward onto the world, onto individuals, or onto groups. In other words, we perceive in the outer world something that belongs to our inner world, and react accordingly. This is why every astrologer must work hard at self-knowledge — without it, they may end up projecting their own personality, motivations, and dramas onto clients, losing sight of the client's actual reality. The most common form of projection is assuming that others feel and act the same way we do.

Projection is also a built-in feature of a natal chart. Every element in a chart is related to the chart's owner — but the planets, signs, and houses also represent the other people and all the situations that person encounters in life: friends, parents, children, partners, pets, work, school, church, and so on.

---

## 16. Your Client Matters More Than the Chart

The word "interpret" means to explain, translate, clarify, or draw out meaning — with the prefix *inter* meaning "between" or "in the middle." Essentially, an astrologer is a mediator and interpreter, a go-between for the client and the chart — a decoder of the chart. The deeper an astrologer's understanding of the symbolic language, the more clearly they can explain it, and the more the client can understand the meaning of their own life. Beginners inevitably feel overwhelmed by so many symbols, or uncertain about how to conduct a reading smoothly — but confidence grows with each consultation, and ease gradually follows.

Because the astrologer is using a chart to help a person — whether paid or as a voluntary service — **the focus must be on the client, not the chart**. An astrologer who can ask the right questions and engage naturally with what the client says, rather than simply delivering a prepared monologue of interpretations, will generally achieve far better results. There are many different approaches to chart interpretation, but regardless of direction, insights can emerge from the client's own chart, the astrologer's chart, chart comparisons, and the chart cast for the time of the consultation.

Some clients — for a variety of reasons (shyness, a desire to test or debunk astrology, wanting to gauge the astrologer's skill) — may remain silent or refuse to share any information, simply listening as the astrologer speaks. But most people will, at some point, begin to engage with their chart, and the atmosphere will relax. No matter how silent a client is, there will come a moment when they feel safe enough to open up and explore. The moment when a client most wants to join the conversation is usually when they feel truly understood by the astrologer — and this is when a genuinely therapeutic experience can emerge. Open, unobstructed communication allows both parties to learn something. The prerequisite, however, is that **the astrologer must be capable of listening to the client — rather than constantly trying to prove astrology's accuracy or show off their own skill**.

---

## 17. The Supervisor

Even the most experienced astrologer can learn something from other astrologers. With the help of a colleague or supervisor, one may discover aspects of a chart — or of oneself — that had previously been overlooked. A supervisor can broaden our perspective, help us learn from our mistakes, and give us greater confidence and perceptiveness.
